# Five JOVRA editorial treatments

These are starting treatments, not compulsory timing recipes. The current project’s real footage, safe framing and exact user intent win. The default is no music, no generated narration, real match speed and low effects. A model can select approved cues; it cannot invent a coach or a result.

## Coach’s Cut — “I can see what to practise next”

Open with the player’s stated goal, then a representative original-speed example. Pause after the evidence, use a short reviewed cue, and replay the relevant movement with a labelled slow-motion or freeze annotation. End on one achievable drill linked to the lesson. Avoid a generic montage that looks athletic but teaches nothing.

Keep the wide view for point context; use a genuine synchronized closer view only when it clarifies the cue. Preserve the complete original-speed keeper separately from annotation layers. A freeze is a deliberate explanatory beat outside that keeper, not a fabricated duration of play. Allow natural pauses for reading; test the hold on a phone at the actual text size.

Sound: clean court audio during the example; lower it under optional user-authorized narration. No narration is necessary when an accurate note and replay can explain the cue. Graphics: one arrow/line at a time, user-reviewed wording, provenance for metrics. Closing feeling: capability and a next action, never shame or diagnosis.

## Match Broadcast — “I can follow and relive the match”

Introduce players as entered by the user, date and match format if recorded. Keep point chronology and select a stable wide camera while the ball is live. Show alternate views as replays after the point; end the replay before the next point starts. Mark recording gaps honestly. Use chapters at real game/set boundaries.

A compact score bug uses the event revision valid for that session moment. Server labels appear only if known. Lower thirds are sparse. Natural audio stays continuous across angle cuts to avoid a second impact or clap. Avoid switching to the opposite baseline mid-rally merely because the camera exists; screen direction can reverse. Establish orientation before an alternate view.

Create tension from real sequence and context, not synthetic announcer claims. Closing feeling: a clean record of the match, with final score only when point coverage supports it. A one-angle match with chapters is an excellent fallback.

## Rally Pulse — “That exchange was worth keeping”

Begin with an actual strong exchange or a clearly labelled payoff teaser. Establish enough context to understand the rally, build through the selected sequence, land on the real point outcome and leave a short human or court-sound breath. Duration can be brief, but do not turn separate points into a fake continuous rally.

Energy comes from the ball, footwork, contact sound and faster pacing between points. Beat-aligned transitions are optional; they must not move recorded event timing or hide contact. Optional shake is low intensity and outside evidence frames. Avoid strobing, repeated zooms and effects that obscure the player/ball. Offer a clean alternate with static titles and ordinary cuts.

Music needs a licence/right record for the intended destination; choose court sound when absent. Do not borrow a popular song because it fits the edit. Retain the player’s actual outcome, including a miss. A portrait cutdown requires a checked action-safe reframe; letterbox or keep landscape if ball/player coverage cannot fit.

## Progress Story — “I remember the work, and can assess the change”

Start with a dated goal, show early representative evidence, attach the real lesson/practice notes, then compare recent evidence under compatible conditions. Finish with both what appears improved and what remains to practise. Use the same order and enough context in before/after examples. Select failures as well as successes when the film claims a measured pattern.

Dates, measurement definitions and denominators matter more than decorative charts. For example 12/30 to 18/30 is 40% to 60%, a change of 20 percentage points. This is a fictional illustration until computed from actual events. Do not call it a 20% relative improvement. Different methods, samples or camera conditions require a warning or omission of the comparison.

Sound and movement are reflective: modest transitions, readable comparisons, user reflections if consented. Do not imply a causal coaching effect from two clips. With no comparable metrics, make an honest lesson chronology and describe the player’s own reflection as a reflection.

## Court Memories — “This day mattered to me”

Follow real experience: arrival or an available establishing shot, preparation, match moments, the human highlight, and a quiet close. Racket/string detail, bag, hands, court texture and natural ambience can be real B-roll when supplied. Each shot has a purpose; do not fill every gap with generic tennis stock.

Allow atmosphere to carry the film. Use a consistent warm treatment that preserves skin, court visibility and detail. Natural sound can bridge arrival into preparation and match into the close. A dedication or venue label is optional; private location stays private. No invented hug, crowd, reaction, speech or likeness.

A stylish opening does not need an 8–10 second hook. Match the opening to output length and attention: a quiet real image and short title can suffice. End at a meaningful actual gesture or sound and let it settle. Stock/generated material, when explicitly requested and permitted, is clearly separate from the recorded experience and never substitutes for tennis evidence.

## Shared shot, audio and graphic decisions

**Camera cuts:** choose by visibility, evidence value, continuity and coverage. Show the same reviewed session event when switching cameras. Review drift at start/middle/end and restarts. No view can fill a coverage gap it did not record.

**Sound:** select one continuous primary court bed per point. Alternate camera sound is used intentionally, with alignment and level matching. Avoid mixing multiple delayed copies of the same impact. Narration is consented and grounded; loudness/true-peak targets are configurable and measured, not asserted because a filter ran. Ducking should preserve speech clarity without pumping. An intentionally silent source is valid.

**Motion and titles:** derive energy from the profile. Use allowlisted primitives with bounded intensity and duration; never arbitrary model code. Keep key titles away from score/captions/action. Test readability at phone and tablet sizes, not only on a monitor. Captions preserve actual speech; an inferred phrase remains a draft.

**Grade:** normalize transfer/colour/HDR before matching cameras. Store technical conversion separately from aesthetic grading. Do not copy the source suite’s unavailable LUTs. Check black/white clipping and noisy dusk footage; preserve evidence visibility.

**Cutdowns:** derive source windows and chapters from the approved plan. Reframe deliberately for 9:16/1:1. If a crop cannot include the action, choose a truthful landscape treatment rather than an invented tracking shot. Each output receives its own audio, title, timing, decode and content check.
